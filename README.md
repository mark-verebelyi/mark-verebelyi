## Hi, I'm Mark 👋

Software engineer since 2007, engineering leader since 2022. Based in Copenhagen.

### The engineer side

I'm most comfortable in Java and the other JVM languages (Kotlin, Scala), though I don't shy away from other stacks. Experience has taught me I can pick things up quickly when a project calls for it.

I've spent most of my career on backend systems: distributed, message-driven, event-heavy, running at meaningful scale. Spring and its ecosystem, Kafka, Postgres, Elasticsearch, Kubernetes. Enough AWS and GCP to know what's actually going on under the hood. I lean hard on Domain-Driven Design and CQRS, which have shaped how I think about software more than any framework ever did.

Security is a lasting interest. I've owned the security domain on greenfield SaaS projects, worked with Keycloak, CAS, and Shibboleth, and still treat application security as part of the job rather than someone else's problem.

AI has changed how I build software, and I've been paying serious attention. I've put real time into prompt engineering, agentic workflows, and AI-assisted development. I know these tools well enough to spec work, challenge assumptions, and review what comes out with a critical eye. Years of system design help me spot the gap between impressive-looking output and code that actually holds up.

### The leader side

I moved from staff engineer to engineering manager in 2022, and stepped up to Senior Engineering Manager a few months later. I'm still learning.

I'm a people-centric leader. I care about building a psychologically safe environment where people have room to be vulnerable and bring their whole selves to work. Everything else follows from that.

Direct leadership taught me how to coach, how to run a fair performance review, how to hold a difficult conversation, and how much of the job is really about people rather than technology. Indirect leadership, through another engineering manager, taught me a different set of skills. How to influence without daily presence. How to trust and coach a leader who has their own style. How to keep momentum on cross-team work when you can't be in every room.

I still care about the code. Most of my leverage now comes from clearing paths for other people, not writing the next commit. But I love a good technical brainstorm. A hard problem on the whiteboard, ideas colliding, real tradeoffs to weigh, everyone working toward something that will actually hold up. I can still hold my own in those rooms.

### Off the clock

I read and watch and listen more than I talk about it. Psychology, the human body, society, the human condition, the future, the universe. Almost anything, really. I love understanding how things actually work in reality. Curiosity is the through-line.

Running and the gym keep me sane. Music is non-negotiable. My taste in film and music runs toward the surreal, the sci-fi, the dark, the sad. There's something honest in work that doesn't try to cheer you up.

### What we're building now

Alongside my engineering career, my wife and I run [FutureColleague.dev](https://futurecolleague.dev/), a specialist recruitment partner for Danish startups and scale-ups. The premise: Hiring Managers, Not Headhunters. Every candidate we present has been through a real technical conversation with a peer, not a keyword scan.

We started this because between us we've sat on every side of the hiring table. As the engineer being pursued, as hiring managers reviewing piles of loosely matched CVs, as people navigating a new work culture. The standard recruitment model has never quite fit any of those seats.
